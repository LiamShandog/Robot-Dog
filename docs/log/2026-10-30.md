## What I did
- Went over pinned vs free torso. Pinned means the torso has no joint, so it is welded to the world and only the legs move. Free means it has a `<freejoint/>` (6 DOF), so it can fall and only the feet hold it up. A middle option is planar slide + hinge joints, which is the sim version of the single-leg boom (sub-project F).
- **Decided: free torso** for the first pass, so I can see the robot as a whole.
- Asked whether the knee could use a smaller motor than the hip. In stance, joint torque = foot force × the *horizontal* distance from the joint to the foot. With the foot roughly under the hip, the hip's moment arm is near zero and the knee carries more torque. So the knee shouldn't get the smaller motor.
- Read two articles on hip vs knee loading and wrote them up in `docs/reading-notes.md`:
  - humanoid.guide says "the hip dominates", but its reasons only apply to humanoids (hip roll in single-leg support, holding up an upright torso). Its own FAQ says the knee usually has the highest published number.
  - The BXI Robotics blog says the knee is usually the most loaded joint "due to its longer moment arm", and uses one motor for both hips and knees.
- Went through what `sim/scene.xml` contains: `compiler` (`angle="radian"`, `inertiafromgeom="auto"`), `option` (2 ms timestep = 500 Hz, gravity), and the floor and lighting.
- Learned the three ways to run a MuJoCo sim (interactive viewer, Python script with `mj_step`, headless) and what `MjModel` and `MjData` are. Checked that the current scene loads and steps headless (`nbody 1`, `njnt 0`, `nu 0`). Added a "Running the sim" section with the viewer command and controls to `sim/README.md`.
- Looked for open-source quadruped models to use as references. Chose to **read them but write my own**: copying one would mean swapping numbers without knowing why, and its masses and geometry come from different hardware.
- Downloaded two reference models to `~/robot_dog/reference-models/`, outside the repo (no third-party licenses in the repo, no mixing them up with my model, no meshes in git):
  - **ODRI Solo-8** (`Gepetto/example-robot-data`, BSD 2-Clause): closest match to my robot (8 DOF, planar legs, similar size).
  - **Unitree Go2** (`mujoco_menagerie`, BSD 3-Clause): an example of good MJCF practice (`<default>` classes, foot contacts, position actuators, robot/scene file split).
- Converted Solo-8 from URDF to MJCF: rewrote the `package://` mesh paths, added a dummy root link and `floating` joint so the torso isn't welded to the world, and saved it with `mj_saveLastXML` as `solo8.xml` with a `solo8_scene.xml` around it. Opened both models in the viewer.

## What broke / surprised me
- MJCF uses **degrees** by default. Without `angle="radian"`, a joint range copied from a radian-based example would be off by a factor of 57.
- With `inertiafromgeom="auto"`, a geom with no `mass` gets its mass from density, which defaults to 1000 kg/m³ (water). Forgetting to set masses gives link masses that have nothing to do with the mass budget.
- When MuJoCo loads a URDF, it welds the top-level link to the world, so Solo-8 came in pinned until a floating root joint was added.
- Solo-8 has no actuators and its URDF joint limits are ±10 rad, so it collapses when the sim plays.
- The 2 ms sim step (500 Hz) is slower than the real 1 kHz joint loop.
