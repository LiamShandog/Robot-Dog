# 2026-10-29 · A/B · Downsizing to 8 DOF around affordable motors
Time spent: 3h

## Goal for this session
Find a motor I can afford for every joint, then size the robot around it.

## What I did
- Found that ~100 Kv motors (needed for decision 0003 revision 1) are too expensive for 13 actuators. Set a cost limit and decided to size the robot down instead.
- Looked for the best motor under ~$40 CAD: the RCTimer 5010 260KV (~$22 CAD, 50 × 10 mm stator, ~100 g, 20 A continuous).
- Reverse-sized from that motor: Kt ≈ 0.032 Nm/A, 0.64 Nm at 20 A, 5.15 Nm at the knee (9:1, 90% efficiency). With 0.15 m legs (knee moment arm ≈ 0.107 m) and 3× trot load, the robot can weigh at most ~3.27 kg.
- Made a mass budget for a 12-DOF robot: ~3.8 kg, which is over the limit.
- Asked about removing the knee to save actuators. The knee can't go: it carries the stance load and lifts the foot during swing. Hip ab/adduction is the joint that can be removed.
- Chose 8 DOF with planar legs, using ODRI Solo-8 as the reference design (inspiration and part choices only; every part designed by me): [decision 0005](../decisions/0005-8-dof-planar-legs.md).
- Re-sized: 9:1, ~300 Kv outrunner, 0.15 m legs, ~2.5-3 kg target: [decision 0003 revision 2](../decisions/0003-gear-ratio-and-sizing.md).
- Replaced the $40/motor cap with a total motor budget under $1,000 CAD.
- Updated README (scope, success criteria, design targets, phase counts, budget, risks), CLAUDE.md and the sub-project READMEs for 8 DOF.
- GPU check (Phase 0): `nvidia-smi` was failing. Root cause was Secure Boot rejecting the DKMS-signed nvidia kernel module (`Key was rejected by service`) because the local MOK signing key was never enrolled. Fixed with `mokutil --import` + enroll at reboot.
- Sim stack decision: compared Isaac Lab, MuJoCo/MJX and Gazebo for sub-projects A/H. Chose MuJoCo/MJX — no vision in scope so Isaac's differentiator doesn't apply, MuJoCo's contact solver fits the push-recovery criterion better, and it fits the 8GB VRAM budget better than PhysX. Written up in [decision 0002](../decisions/0002-sim-stack.md).
- Talked through whether ROS2 belongs in the control/deploy stack. Decided it doesn't, for now: the actuator network is a fixed set of 8 nodes doing distributed FOC (local 10kHz current loop per actuator MCU) with CAN carrying only the 1kHz joint loop, and the 50Hz policy sits on top — a deterministic, known-topology problem, not the loosely-coupled dynamic-node-graph problem ROS2/DDS is built for. ROS2 would make more sense later for perception/navigation if that's ever added, but that's out of this project's scope.
- Sanity-checked onboard compute for the RL policy: inference (not training) is the relevant number for sub-project G, and legged-locomotion policies are small MLPs (sub-millisecond forward pass) — a Pi 5-class CPU is enough, no GPU/Jetson needed unless vision gets added later.
- Found a Cobra CM-4510/28 (420 Kv, 35 A continuous, 45×10 mm stator, 211 g) as free surplus in the aerospace bay — 8 available. Reverse-sized it the same way as the RCTimer/T-Motor rows in [0003](../decisions/0003-gear-ratio-and-sizing.md): 0.69 Nm motor torque, 5.58 Nm at the knee, max robot mass ~3.54 kg at 9:1 and 3× trot — better than the RCTimer's 3.27 kg on raw torque.
- Redid the mass budget with the Cobra's actual 211 g (vs the ~100 g assumed for the RCTimer): actuator mass rises to ~336 g each (~2.69 kg for 8), pushing estimated robot mass to ~3.79 kg — above its own 3.54 kg torque ceiling. Torque-per-gram (0.0033 Nm/g vs the RCTimer's 0.0064 Nm/g) shows the Cobra is worse for the final build despite the higher raw torque number.
- Decision: use the free Cobras for actuator v1 bring-up and the single-leg test rig (sub-project F runs on a boom, so mass budget doesn't bind there) — keep final motor choice for the full 8-actuator build open for 0001.

## What broke / surprised me
- GPU driver install looked fine (`dkms status` said installed) but Secure Boot silently blocked the module from loading — nothing showed in `dmesg`, had to check `journalctl` for the actual rejection reason.
- Solo-8's T-Motor MN4004 300KV is ~$72 each, nearly double the original per-motor limit.
- The cheaper RCTimer 5010 has a much bigger stator (D² × h ≈ 25,000 vs 6,400 for the 4004), so on paper it should give more torque. What it costs is weight: ~100 g vs ~50-60 g.
- Solo-8 uses a two-stage timing belt (3:1 × 3:1), not a planetary gearbox. A belt keeps the loaded teeth off printed parts, which matters with PLA.
- Dropping hip ab/ad removes sideways walking and weakens turning and sideways push recovery, so the success criteria changed.
- The free Cobra motor looked better than the RCTimer on raw max-supportable-mass (3.54 kg vs 3.27 kg), until its own weight was fed back into the mass budget — then it came out worse. Torque per gram, not raw torque, is the number that matters once the motor's weight is a meaningful fraction of the robot.

## What I learned
DC motors are expensive. It's a good idea to center your design around these components that you must order. You can always alter the robot chassis, and increase gear ratio, but it's not easy to get new motors.
